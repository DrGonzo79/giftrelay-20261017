# Office Hours product diagnostic

## Specific customer
Austin executive assistants/chiefs of staff at 50–500 person companies who handle urgent gifts several times a month and personally absorb the reputational cost of a generic or late choice.

## Painful status quo
They search generic catalogs, call shops individually, guess at live inventory, coordinate a courier, and still cannot prove the gift feels personal. The pain is deadline compression plus taste risk, not “finding products.”

## Desperate use case
An executive says at 10 a.m.: “A client closes today at 4; $150; tasteful, local, no alcohol.” The EA needs three credible, deliverable options within 15 minutes.

## Narrowest paid wedge
One Austin neighborhood, weekday business hours, curated home/food/design gifts, $75–$250 orders. Charge the reported $9 brief fee only after three boutiques reliably answer inside 15 minutes; manually dispatch the first 30 orders. Do not start with consumer discovery, nationwide supply, or open-ended AI recommendations.

## Observation and validation
- Shadow 10 EAs through their most recent urgent gift; collect timestamps, calls, substitutions and budget.
- Concierge 20 live briefs over two weeks; log response latency, choice rate, on-time delivery and refunds.
- Ask for the $9 fee before marketplace automation. Success: 8 paid briefs, 70% get 2+ eligible responses in 15 minutes, 80% on-time, 30% repeat intent. Kill/pivot if paid conversion is below 20% after 40 qualified briefs or supply fill below 50%.

## Future fit
If the brief ledger becomes trusted, expand into recurring recipient memory, consolidated company billing, and boutique demand forecasting. AI can parse briefs and flag repetition; it should not invent inventory or delivery promises.
