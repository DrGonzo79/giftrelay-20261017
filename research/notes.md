## Customer and pain

Initial buyer: executive assistants and chiefs of staff who must source a thoughtful, non-repetitive gift on a short deadline. Adjacent buyer: a spouse/friend who forgot an occasion. The status quo is frantic generic browsing, unreliable local inventory, or expensive corporate platforms. Public demand receipts cited urgent last-minute willingness to pay and EAs describing gifts as unusually hard.

## Wedge and why now

The narrow wedge is **one urgent brief → multiple photo-backed local boutique responses → same-day choice**, starting in one gift-dense city. It differs from a conventional catalog: supply responds to intent even when inventory is not digitized. Plain-language shopping behavior is growing, while DoorDash Drive/Uber Direct-style APIs remove courier infrastructure as a prerequisite. IdeaBrowser cited 800M people shopping by chat, a $9.86B US same-day market in 2025, and 80% consumer expectation of same-day delivery.

## Market, competition, and signals

The report mapped 11 players. Whim and Gallop are closest city-marketplace competitors; Dashing in the City sells same-day curated NYC gifts; Giftagram, Sendoso and Goody focus more on corporate programs; 1-800-Flowers and DoorDash own broad same-day reach. The purported whitespace is not gift delivery itself but “briefed local boutique in three hours.” Evidence included a $258B 2022 US corporate gifting benchmark projected to $312B in 2025, consolidation (Sendoso/Alyce/Postal), community complaints, and weak direct SEO volume. Treat market estimates as source claims, not independently verified facts.

## Offer ladder and economics

1. City last-minute gift guide — free lead magnet.
2. Urgent brief — $9 plus 15% of gift; report assumes $120 AOV and $27 platform revenue.
3. EA Pro — $299/month for 10 briefs, priority response, profiles and repeat blocking.
4. Company account — $12,000/year.

Napkin case: 50 Pro seats plus 300 monthly briefs produces about $23,050 MRR by month nine. The 10% close rate and 60% brief utilization are unproven assumptions. Search demand for “gift concierge”/“personal gift shopper” was cited at only 140/170 monthly; first distribution should be EA communities, direct LinkedIn/Reddit outreach, hospitality/event partnerships, and boutique walk-ins—not paid search.

## Risks

- City-by-city liquidity and boutique trust require sustained local sales.
- Whim/Gallop may deepen supply relationships; ChatGPT Shopping/Google UCP may digitize local catalogs.
- Quality, substitution, fraud, allergen and delivery failures become operational liability.
- The marketplace can look liquid in a demo while failing under real deadline pressure.

## Build interpretation

GiftRelay prototypes the riskiest loop: structured buyer brief, deterministic budget/deadline/interest eligibility, transparent ranking, and gift selection. It deliberately avoids fake checkout and labels all supply and delivery state fictional.
